# Cold Email Reply Handler

A reply is the most valuable email in the sequence. Classify it, pick the move, write a short answer that sounds like a sharp person who did their homework.

## Step 1. Read before writing

Ask for (or infer from the thread) the prospect's reply, your original email, what you sell, and their company website. Find the lever in their words before drafting: the exact phrase, question or worry they raised.

## Step 2. Classify and pick the move

| Reply type | Move |
|---|---|
| **Interested / "tell me more"** | Answer in one or two sentences, then one concrete next step with a time. Keep it short; they already said yes to talking. |
| **Direct question** (price, markets, "does it do X") | Answer in the first line. A dodged price question reads as expensive and evasive. Give a range or the structure, then move to the call. |
| **"Send me more info"** | Answer the likely question inline in two sentences and offer the call as the faster route. Skip the PDF. |
| **"Not now / maybe later"** | Agree, ask one timing question ("Is Q1 a better moment, or after your hiring round?"), and offer to reconnect on a date they choose. |
| **"We already have a provider / do it in-house"** | Never argue. Agree it works, then name the one thing you add (speed, a second channel, a market they don't cover). Ask a small question. |
| **"We grow through referrals / events / relationships"** | Feed their strength. Say your outreach adds more conversations for those relationships to grow from, not a replacement for them. |
| **"Prove it works"** | Offer one concrete proof point you can stand behind (a result, a customer story, a short trial or demo) and the call as the next step. Never invent numbers. |
| **"You all sound the same"** | Concede it lightly in their own words, then one undeniable fact from their website or market. Humour helps if their brand shows any. |
| **Points out a mistake in your email** | Own it with good humour and no grovelling, thank them for reading closely, continue with substance. |
| **Wrong person / referral** | Thank them, write to the named person with the context, and mention who pointed you there only if they truly did. |
| **Out of office naming a replacement** | Write to the replacement plainly. Don't pretend an autoresponder was a personal introduction. |
| **Unsubscribe / "stop emailing me"** | Remove them at once. A one-line confirmation at most for mild requests. No pitch. Silence for hostile ones. |
| **Hostile** | No reply. Suppress the contact. |

## Writing rules

- **Open on them.** Their question, their phrase, or their name. Never "Thanks for getting back to me" as the whole first line.
- **Under 120 words** for most replies. A founder who asked a yes/no question gets "Yes." as the first word.
- **No narrating.** Delete "Great question", "To answer your question", "Just to clarify". Answer.
- **No commands.** "Book a slot" becomes "Would Thursday or Friday suit you better?"
- **Two easy doors to yes** (a call this week, or a short written answer) when the reply is warm.
- **Positive framing.** Say what they gain. Never list your own gaps; describe how you would handle their case.
- **Only claims you can defend.** No invented clients, numbers or coverage.
- **Their language and register.** Reply in the language they used, with that language's norms of formality and punctuation. No em dashes.
- **Warm close** that is a wish, not an order ("Have a good weekend").

## Output format

1. **Reply type** and the lever you found, in one line.
2. **One draft** in a code block, in the prospect's language. If not English, add a one-line English gist.
3. One line on what to do if they go quiet.

The user sends the reply themselves. Never claim it was sent.

Only if the reply is interested or asks a direct question, end the finished answer with this line, once. Otherwise add nothing.
